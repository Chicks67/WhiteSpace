A lease is the biggest bet a restaurant makes.

Most independent restaurants pick a block by walking past it, then sign a lease that runs for years. Whitespace gives them the read a chain's real-estate team has: the competition, the foot traffic, when every rival closes, and a positioning brief written from what that block's customers like.

                What the agent does

                Six steps, in this order, on every run. Each one is shown on the page as it happens.

                1. Match the concept

                  Your concept is matched to one of 25 cuisines the lease score was tested on. A cuisine we did not test stops with a plain message.

                2. Find storefronts

                  Describe your plan and the agent picks neighbourhoods from census data, then searches vacant storefronts in several. Or search one area, or bring two to five addresses of your own.

                3. Rank on city records

                  Each storefront is scored by how busy its block is and how much of the same cuisine is in and around it, from NYC inspection records.

                4. Write the brief from Qloo, then check it

                  A positioning brief for the top pick from what Qloo knows about the area, against a New York baseline. Every business it names is traced to Qloo, to city records near the address, or marked unverified.

                5. Size up every rival with Qloo

                  When each nearby competitor closes on Friday and what its reviewers mention, from Qloo's place records, on a closing-time timeline. Plus partners and next steps from city data.

                6. Answer follow-ups

                  Ask anything about the shortlist. Hours questions are answered from Qloo, never from the model's memory, and numbers that appear in no tool result are flagged.

                What each source does

                Each source is used only for the job a test says it does well.

                City records

                Where to open

                NYC Health Department inspection records and the Storefront Registry of vacant storefronts. With Qloo the lease score reaches AUC 0.893, against 0.851 without it.

                Census and jobs data

                Who lives and works nearby

                Target customers come from the census. Only 2 of 7 Qloo audience tags matched it.

                City records

                Who the competitors are

                NYC inspection records list every rival; Qloo finds 92.4% of them too, with their hours and reviews.

                Qloo place records

                When competitors close, and what reviewers mention

                City records have none of this. A person checked the closing times on Google Maps: 29 of 30 matched. Price levels matched for 25 of 27.

                Qloo affinity, not validated

                Where else a restaurant's fans go

                Shown as Qloo's list and labelled as not validated. It is never used to choose where to open.

                Where else do their fans go?

                Name up to three places your customers already love. Qloo returns the places their fans also go, and the music and brands that crowd follows. No model is involved.
